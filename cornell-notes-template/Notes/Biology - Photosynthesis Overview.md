# Biology - Photosynthesis Overview

> [!cue] Cues
> - What is photosynthesis, in one line?
> - Inputs and outputs?
> - Where in the cell?
> - Two stages — what splits them?
> - Why does it matter beyond the plant?

> [!notes] Notes
> **One-line idea:** plants, algae and some bacteria use light energy to build sugars from carbon dioxide and water, releasing oxygen. It converts light energy into chemical energy stored in molecules.
>
> **Inputs → outputs (descriptive, no equations)**
> - In: light, carbon dioxide (from air, via stomata), water (from roots).
> - Out: glucose (chemical-energy store), oxygen (released as by-product).
>
> **Where.** In the chloroplasts. The green pigment *chlorophyll* absorbs light — strongly in red and blue, poorly in green, which is why leaves look green (green is reflected).
>
> **Two stages**
> 1. *Light-dependent reactions* — in the thylakoid membranes. Light is captured; water is split (this is where the released oxygen comes from); short-term energy carriers are charged up.
> 2. *Light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle)* — in the stroma. The captured energy is used to fix carbon dioxide into sugar. No light needed directly, but it depends on the carriers from stage one.
>
> **Bigger picture.** Roughly the reverse partner of respiration: photosynthesis stores energy and releases oxygen, respiration releases that energy and uses oxygen. Base of most food chains and the source of atmospheric oxygen.

> [!summary] Summary
> Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy, using carbon dioxide and water to make glucose and releasing oxygen. It runs in chloroplasts in two linked stages: light-dependent reactions in the thylakoids (capturing light, splitting water) and the Calvin cycle in the stroma (building sugar). It underpins most food chains and supplies atmospheric oxygen.

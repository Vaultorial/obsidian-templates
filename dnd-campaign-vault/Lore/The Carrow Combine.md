# The Carrow Combine

The great merchant house of [[Vellharrow]]: warehouses, lighters, insurance shares and a counting floor that prices half the Brinemark's trade. Publicly, the Combine is the road's best friend — it funds festival processions and laments every lost wagon. Privately, it is the author of the losses.

## What it is

Three generations of the Carrow family turned a weighhouse stall into a house that owns pieces of everything: cargo space, warehouse keys, debt paper, magistrates' dinner invitations. [[Iselle Carrow]] inherited a strong house and decided strong was not the same as safe — safe is owning the source. The pans, the road, the harbor: vertical, hers.

## How the scheme works

1. **Stage the danger.** Hired crews "vanish" wagons at Miller's Cut; the wagons are repainted and absorbed into Combine fleets. Cheap, reusable, and the drovers' disappearances do the advertising.
2. **Let fear do the buying.** Insurers refuse the route; independents can't ship uninsured; salt piles up unsold at [[The Greyflat Pans]].
3. **Arrive as the rescuer.** Combine factors offer to buy the pans at a "fair price under the circumstances" — circumstances the Combine manufactured.
4. **Own the protection.** [[Hesk Adler]]'s retainer keeps patrols pointed the wrong way and Warden seals available for ledger work by [[Dorrik Vane]].

## Its people

- [[Iselle Carrow]] — the will. Distant from every mechanism, deniable by design.
- [[Dorrik Vane]] — the memory. Both ledgers, one frightened man.
- The Vellharrow fixer (unnamed; [[Joss Brell]] sells the name for a favor) — the hands that hire hands.

## Cracks in the house

- Paper. The scheme is efficient because it is administered, and administration leaves ledgers — see [[Counting House Break-In]].
- The saltwights. Desecration was never in the plan; it offends even Combine hirelings, and it gave the pansmen a grievance no purse settles.
- Dorrik Vane's nerve, which Iselle has priced too cheaply.

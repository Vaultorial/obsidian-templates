# The Salt Road

Four days of cart road between [[Saltmere]] on the coast and [[Vellharrow]] in the market hills, with [[Hollowford Waystation]] at the ford halfway. Everything the Brinemark is — its towns, its tolls, its law, its current war — exists because salt has to travel this road.

## Why salt rules everything

The Brinemark's salt preserves the catch of an entire coast and the slaughter of the inland farms. Whoever controls the road controls what every smokehouse and salting cellar pays — which is why the road was deliberately made hard to own: pans held in common by the pansmen, tolls held in trust by [[The Brine Wardens]], the ledger kept by the waystation's charter. Three institutions, none of which was supposed to be buyable.

[[The Carrow Combine]] found the fourth thing: fear. Make the road look lawless and the insurers do the conquering for you.

## How the road works

- **Wagons and waybills.** Every cargo carries a manifest; the waystation stamps it; the Wardens inspect it. Amending a stamped page requires Warden seals — the design assumed the Wardens were incorruptible.
- **The toll.** Collected at Hollowford, meant to fund the Warden patrols. Years of thin traffic left the toll fund short, which is the crack [[Hesk Adler]]'s pension fears grew through.
- **The waystations.** Hollowford is the great one; smaller posts mark the dry stages. Charter law makes them neutral ground — even now, nobody has dared break that openly.

## The road today

Three wagons "vanished" in two months (staged at Miller's Cut), insurers refusing the route, the pans under a forced-sale offer, and the road's sworn protector owned by the buyer. The conspiracy in one sentence: [[Iselle Carrow]] is buying the road by making it look like the road is dying.

## Table reference

- Saltmere to Hollowford: two days by wagon, one hard day by horse.
- Hollowford to Vellharrow: two days by wagon through open hill country.
- Miller's Cut lies a half-day east of Hollowford — see [[Ambush at Miller's Cut]].

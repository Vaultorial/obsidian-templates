# Lecture 02 - DNA and Genes

## Notes

**What DNA is.** The molecule carrying hereditary information, shaped as a double helix — two strands twisted together. Building blocks are nucleotides, each with one of four bases: adenine (A), thymine (T), guanine (G), cytosine (C).

**Base pairing.** The strands are complementary: A pairs with T, G pairs with C. So one strand's sequence determines the other's — the structural basis for faithful copying.

**Gene → protein, in plain terms**
- A *gene* is a stretch of DNA that codes for a product, usually a protein.
- *Transcription* — the gene's DNA sequence is copied into messenger RNA.
- *Translation* — ribosomes read the mRNA and assemble the matching chain of amino acids into a protein.
- Shorthand for the overall flow: DNA → RNA → protein.

**Why it matters.** Proteins do most of the cell's work (structure, enzymes, signaling from [[Lecture 01 - The Cell]]), so the DNA sequence ultimately shapes what a cell can do.

# Lecture 03 - Cellular Respiration

## Notes

**The big idea.** Cells break down nutrients (chiefly glucose) to release stored chemical energy into a usable form the cell can spend. Described in lecture as roughly the partner process to photosynthesis: respiration releases energy and consumes oxygen, photosynthesis stores energy and releases oxygen.

**Inputs and outputs (descriptive, no equations)**
- In: glucose and oxygen.
- Out: carbon dioxide, water, and usable energy.

**Where it happens.** Largely in the *mitochondria* — the organelle introduced in [[Lecture 01 - The Cell]] as the energy releaser. This lecture is the detail behind that one-liner.

**Aerobic vs. anaerobic**
- *Aerobic* respiration uses oxygen and releases comparatively more usable energy per glucose.
- *Anaerobic* pathways run without oxygen and release less; lecture's everyday example was muscle cells during hard exertion switching to a less efficient route when oxygen runs short.

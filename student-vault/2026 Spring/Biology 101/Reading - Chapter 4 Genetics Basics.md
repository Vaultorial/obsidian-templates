## My summary

The chapter introduces classical inheritance — how traits pass from parents to offspring — using simple single-gene examples. The core vocabulary: a *gene* can occur in different versions called *alleles*; an organism carries two alleles per gene, one from each parent. *Genotype* is the pair of alleles present; *phenotype* is the observable trait that results. When one allele's effect masks the other's, the masking version is *dominant* and the masked one *recessive*. It connects back to [[Lecture 02 - DNA and Genes]]: alleles are just alternative DNA sequences of the same gene.

## Key terms (own words)

- **Allele** — one of the alternative versions of a gene.
- **Genotype** — the alleles an organism has; **phenotype** — the trait you can actually observe.
- **Dominant / recessive** — whether an allele's effect shows when paired with a different allele.
- **Homozygous / heterozygous** — the two alleles are the same / different.

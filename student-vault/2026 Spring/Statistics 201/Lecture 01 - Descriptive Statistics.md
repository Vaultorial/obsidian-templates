# Lecture 01 - Descriptive Statistics

## Notes

**Purpose.** Describe a dataset compactly without listing every value. Two jobs: locate the center and measure the spread.

**Measures of center**
- *Mean* — sum divided by count; uses every value, sensitive to extremes.
- *Median* — the middle value when ordered; resistant to extremes.
- *Mode* — most frequent value; useful for categorical data.

**Measures of spread**
- *Range* — maximum minus minimum; set entirely by the two extreme points.
- *Variance / standard deviation* — typical distance of values from the mean. Standard deviation is in the same units as the data, which is why it is the one usually reported.

**Skew and the mean–median gap.** On a long-tailed distribution the mean is pulled toward the tail while the median stays near the bulk. A mean noticeably above the median signals a right-skew (a few large values).

## Questions to review

- What two things does a descriptive summary always report?
- Mean vs. median — which resists extremes, and why?
- Standard deviation vs. variance — why report the standard deviation?
- What does a mean well above the median tell you about shape?

# Reading - Intro to Probability

**Assigned reading due:** 2026-02-16 (on the [[Dashboard]]).
**Source:** course textbook, Chapter 5.

## My summary

The chapter formalizes the intuitions from [[Lecture 02 - Probability Basics]]. It frames probability two ways: the *theoretical* view (equally likely outcomes, counted directly — a fair coin gives one-half by symmetry) and the *empirical* view (the long-run fraction of times an event occurs over many repeated trials). It introduces the idea that, as trials accumulate, the observed fraction tends to settle near the theoretical probability — the everyday meaning of "the law of large numbers", described qualitatively here without proof.

## Key terms (own words)

- **Theoretical probability** — computed from the structure of the situation (symmetry, counting).
- **Empirical probability** — estimated from observed data: successes ÷ trials.
- **Law of large numbers (informal)** — more trials, and the empirical estimate tends to close in on the theoretical value.

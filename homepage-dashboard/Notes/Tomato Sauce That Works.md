# Tomato Sauce That Works

The house sauce. Written down after the third time of trying to remember what made batch two so good. Scales linearly; freezes well in jam jars.

## Ingredients (two generous jars)

- 1 kg ripe tomatoes, roughly chopped — the dream is balcony-grown, see [[Balcony Garden Summer Setup]]
- 1 onion, halved (not chopped)
- 2 cloves of garlic, squashed flat
- A real knob of butter and a pour of olive oil
- Salt, one pinch of sugar only if the tomatoes sulk
- Basil added at the end, never boiled

## Method

1. Everything except the basil into the widest pan owned, medium-low heat.
2. Forty-five minutes, mostly ignored, stirred when passing. The onion does the work; fish it out at the end.
3. Crush with the wooden spoon. Chunky is correct; blending is for emergencies.
4. Off the heat: tear in the basil, taste, salt again, done.

## Field notes

- Batch two's secret turned out to be patience, not a secret ingredient. Annoying but true.
- Doubles as the base for shakshuka-style eggs on reset Sundays — see [[Weekly Reset Checklist]].
- Jar labels matter: "June, good batch" tells future-me nothing. Date + tomato source.

# How to Take Smart Notes

## Summary

An argument that writing is where thinking happens, wrapped around the Zettelkasten method of sociologist Niklas Luhmann. Ahrens' target is the standard workflow of highlighting, collecting and then facing a blank page: he proposes the inverse, where every piece of reading immediately becomes a small permanent note in your own words, linked into a growing web — so "writing something" later is assembly of thinking already done, never creation from nothing. The method is simple; the book's real work is dismantling the habits that make it feel unnecessary.

## Key ideas

- A note in your own words is a comprehension test; a highlight is a deferral of one.
- One idea per note, linked to existing notes — the links are where new thinking accumulates.
- Work bottom-up: let topics emerge from clusters of notes rather than collecting toward a predecided thesis.
- Standardize the boring parts (formats, IDs, one inbox) so the interesting parts get all the attention.

## Favorite quote

Skipped on purpose — the ideas paraphrase better than they quote, which is rather the book's point.

## What changed because of it

Reading now ends with two or three permanent notes instead of a page of highlights I never revisit. Slow in the moment, dramatically better three months later.

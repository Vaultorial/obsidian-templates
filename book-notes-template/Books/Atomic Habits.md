# Atomic Habits

## Summary

A mechanics manual for behavior change built on one premise: outcomes follow systems, so work on the system and let the outcomes arrive on their own schedule. Clear decomposes every habit into cue, craving, response and reward, then gives a lever for each — make it obvious, attractive, easy, satisfying (and invert all four to break a habit). The book's quieter, better idea is identity: each repetition is a small vote for being the kind of person who does this, which explains why tiny actions compound psychologically and not just arithmetically.

## Key ideas

- Design the environment instead of relying on willpower — put the cue in your path, put the friction on the bad option.
- Habit stacking: attach the new behavior to an existing one ("after I X, I will Y").
- Make it small enough to be unskippable on a bad day; the floor matters more than the ceiling.
- Never miss twice — one lapse is an accident, two is the start of a new habit.

## Favorite quote

> "You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems."

## What changed because of it

The "never miss twice" rule quietly fixed my exercise consistency where streak-counting had repeatedly failed it. A 4 not a 5 only because the core fits in a long essay — but it is the essay I keep applying.

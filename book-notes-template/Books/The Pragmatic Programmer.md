# The Pragmatic Programmer

## Summary

In progress — about halfway. A book of working philosophy for software people, structured as a long series of compact, numbered tips rather than a single argument. What distinguishes it so far is the constant return to *attitude*: take responsibility for what you ship, fix small rots early, invest deliberately in your own toolkit and knowledge. Reading the 20th anniversary edition.

## Key ideas (so far)

- Don't live with broken windows — small neglected flaws normalize decay, in codebases and elsewhere.
- DRY is about knowledge, not text: every piece of knowledge should have one authoritative home.
- Orthogonality: design parts so a change in one cannot ripple into the others.
- Be a catalyst for change — "start with stone soup": demonstrate a small working version and let people add to it.

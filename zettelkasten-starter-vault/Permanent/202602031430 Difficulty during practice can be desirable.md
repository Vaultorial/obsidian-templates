# Difficulty during practice can be desirable

Learning research uses the term *desirable difficulties* for conditions that make practice feel harder while making the resulting memory stronger — spacing sessions apart, testing instead of reviewing, mixing topics instead of blocking them. The shared mechanism: each one forces the brain to do more reconstruction work per repetition, and reconstruction is what builds durable recall.

The word *desirable* carries a warning, though. Difficulty helps only when it is the kind the learner can still overcome; confusion from bad materials or missing prerequisites is just difficulty, with nothing desirable about it. The test I use: hard because I am being made to retrieve and connect — good; hard because I cannot tell what is being asked — bad.

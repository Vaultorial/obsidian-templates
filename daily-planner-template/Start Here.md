# Start Here

Welcome. This vault is a time-blocking day planner: every day gets three priorities (the **Top 3**), a timeline of blocks in Day Planner format, and a two-minute **Shutdown** at the end. Three filled days and a week overview are included so you can see a real, imperfect week — including blocks that got moved.

## How the vault is organized

- `Planner/` — one note per day (`YYYY-MM-DD`) plus a simple weekly overview note (`Week of 2026-06-08`). The examples cover Monday to Wednesday, June 8–10, 2026; Wednesday is intentionally mid-day, with the afternoon still unchecked.
- `Templates/` — `Daily Planner` (applied automatically to new daily notes) and `Weekly Overview`.

The core **Daily notes** plugin is configured to create planner notes in `Planner/` from the template.

## The method, in four rules

1. **Top 3 first.** If only these three things happen, the day was good. Pick them before looking at your calendar.
2. **Block the timeline.** Use `- [ ] 09:00 - 10:30 Deep work: thing` lines under *Day planner*. Blocks are bets, not promises — move them when reality interferes, don't abandon the list.
3. **One deep block before lunch.** Protect it like a client meeting.
4. **Shutdown, always.** Tick what happened, write what carries to tomorrow, close the laptop. The shutdown is what makes tomorrow's planning take five minutes instead of thirty.

## First steps

1. Read the three example days in order — Monday went to plan, Tuesday did not, Wednesday is in progress. That contrast is the point.
2. Open today's daily note (calendar ribbon icon or `Open today's daily note`). The template loads itself.
3. Write your Top 3, block the morning, start.

## Plugins

- **Day Planner** (optional but recommended) — turns the timeline lines into a visual schedule with a "now" marker in the sidebar, and shows your day as a timeline. Install it, keep its defaults, and it will pick up the `- [ ] HH:mm - HH:mm` tasks from your daily notes automatically.

Everything else is core plugins, already configured.

> [!tip] Estimate in halves
> If you are new to time blocking, make every block half as ambitious as feels right. Two honest 90-minute deep blocks beat a fantasy schedule that collapses at 10:15.

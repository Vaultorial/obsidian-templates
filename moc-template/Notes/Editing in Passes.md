# Editing in Passes

Up: [[Writing MOC]]

Edit one concern at a time, in order of expense: structure first (does the argument hold, is anything missing or doubled), then paragraphs and flow, then sentences, then a final pass for typos and tics. Trying to do everything at once means doing the cheap work on material the structural pass was about to delete — polished paragraphs are the hardest ones to cut, and the cut is usually right.

Each pass wants a different brain: read the structural pass as a skeptic, the sentence pass aloud. Between passes, distance — even a day changes what is visible.

Related: [[Outlining Before Drafting]] prevents most structural surgery; [[Freewriting]] explains why the draft is allowed to be bad in the first place; [[Writing for One Reader]] sharpens the question every pass keeps asking — clear *to whom*?

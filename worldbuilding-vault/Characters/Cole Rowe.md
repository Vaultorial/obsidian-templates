# Cole Rowe

Warden of the Bells for thirty years; the man who survived the Breaking and has spent the rest of his life making sure the sea never catches anyone on his stones again.

## Appearance and manner

Sixties, weathered to driftwood, walks the causeway daily in any weather. Speaks rarely and in bell-terms: situations are "rising", people are "mid-peal". Wears the cracked clapper of the old Third Tower bell on a cord, and everyone knows better than to ask.

## Wants and fears

- Stated want: bell-law obeyed to the letter. The letter is written in the names of his drowned crew.
- Real want: to retire having never lost a walker — and lately, guiltily, to understand why the tides have started arriving off the old tables. He has noticed. Wardens always notice first.
- Fear: being ordered to use the causeway against Lowmark — closing it, or holding it under arms — when the Drowning comes. He serves the Court; he buried Lowmark friends in 368.

## Relationships

- [[Elowen Hark]] — his charter authority; mutual, wordless respect between two people married to their duties.
- [[Bren Callow]] — Floodsworn runners cross Rowe's stones at the peal's edge. His log of them is mysteriously slow to reach [[Ostreve]], which is as political as Cole Rowe gets.
- [[Wick the Ferryman]] — opposite numbers and old friends: one rules the dry crossing, one the wet, and they have divided the bay's safety between them for decades.

## Appears in

- [[The Breaking of the Bells]] — the young survivor who rebuilt the watch.
- [[The Drowning of Lowmark]] — whoever moves that day, moves across his causeway.

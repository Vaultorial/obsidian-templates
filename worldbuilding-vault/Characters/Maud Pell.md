# Maud Pell

Keeper of Charts of the Drowned Bell — the person who discovered that the sea has stopped agreeing with the law, and has not yet decided whom to tell.

## Appearance and manner

Fifties, sea-booted under her habit, fingers permanently ink-and-brine. Talks to instruments politely, to officials less so. Writes everything twice: once as measured, once as it will be heard.

## Wants and fears

- Stated want: honest soundings, sealed and delivered, as the Order has vowed for three centuries.
- Real want: a way to publish the silt findings that reforms tide-law instead of shattering it — she has seen what happens to bearers of unwelcome measurements.
- Fear: that delay is itself a falsification. Every sealed letter she does not send to [[Elowen Hark]] feels heavier than the last.

## Relationships

- [[Elowen Hark]] — thirty years of chart-letters; the friendship is real and is exactly what makes the truth hard to send.
- [[Perrin Lowsmith]] — the blight survey bound them; his dead moss-beds are her data and his grief, and she keeps both distinctions carefully.
- The old abbot — nominal superior, failing; in practice the Order already follows Maud.

## Appears in

- [[The Mirrormoor Blight]] — traced the blight to shifted silt lines; the finding beneath every other plot in this vault.
- [[The Drowning of Lowmark]] — if her corrections reach the Court before the vote, the law itself comes into question.

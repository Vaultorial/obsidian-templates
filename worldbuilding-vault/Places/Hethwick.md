# Hethwick

A day's climb from the bay, where the air stops tasting of salt: timber-framed market squares, wool and barley country, and the pleasant smugness of a town whose streets have never flooded. Hethwick measures wealth in dry goods and considers the tide a coastal eccentricity.

## What this place is

The Tide Courts' landward door and the only sizable town outside tide-law — it answers to its own market council and to no chart. That independence makes Hethwick everyone's neutral ground: Ebb Court factors, Floodsworn pamphleteers and Order purchasing-brothers all lodge on the same square and pretend not to know each other.

The press matters most. What cannot be said in [[Lowmark]] without a warden hearing is printed in Hethwick by the hundredweight and carried down the upland road in fish-barrels.

## People here

- No major character calls Hethwick home, which is itself the point: people come here to be away from where they are known. [[Bren Callow]] prints here; [[Verity Ostreve]]'s discreet land purchases are notarized here.

## Tensions

- The market council profits from Lowmark's troubles (refugee labor is cheap, pamphlets sell) and dreads them (a drowned port means no fish, no moss, no buyers).
- If [[The Drowning of Lowmark]] breaks into open conflict, Hethwick's neutrality becomes a side, whether it likes it or not.

## Connections

- Upland road down to [[Lowmark]]; caravan routes inland that make it the moss trade's last Tide Courts stop.
- Where the Floodsworn's broadsheets are printed and where, rumor says, Verity's bolt-hole estate lies.

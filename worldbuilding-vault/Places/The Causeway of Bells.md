# The Causeway of Bells

Three miles of wave-worn stone between [[Ostreve]] and [[Lowmark]], dry for a few hours in every tide and gone the rest. Bell towers stand the whole length of it, each tuned differently, so a walker in fog knows by ear which tower is near and how long the sea will let them live.

## What this place is

The only road between the two cities that does not need a boat — which makes it the Tide Courts' artery, chokepoint and law in one. Bell-law governs the crossing: when the descending peal sounds, you turn back, whoever you are and whatever you carry. The towers are kept by causeway wardens under Ebb Court charter, led for thirty years by [[Cole Rowe]].

## People here

- [[Cole Rowe]] — Warden of the Bells, survivor of the crossing that killed his crew.

## Tensions

- Toll revenue flows uphill to Ostreve while repair costs stay with the wardens; three towers still carry storm-cracks from 368.
- Floodsworn runners cross at the edge of the peal to dodge toll and notice. Rowe's wardens have orders to log them; Rowe himself has been slow to forward the log.

## Connections

- Joins [[Ostreve]] and [[Lowmark]]; when it floods, traffic falls to [[Wick the Ferryman]].
- Site of [[The Breaking of the Bells]] in 368 TR, the disaster that made bell-law what it is.

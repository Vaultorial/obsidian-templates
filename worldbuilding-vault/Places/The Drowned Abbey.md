# The Drowned Abbey

From the moor's northern horn at neap tide you can walk to it: a half-sunk abbey on a tidal island, its lower cloister given to the sea generations ago, its bell tower still dry and still ringing soundings. The monks did not abandon the flooded halls — they shelved them. Wax-sealed chart-cases line walls that fish swim past.

## What this place is

Mother house of the [[Order of the Drowned Bell]] and the archive of the Tide Courts: three centuries of tide-readings, storm registers and silt surveys, the raw record beneath every chart [[The Ebb Court]] rules by. The abbey accepts visitors who arrive on foot — by the sea's leave, as the monks put it — which keeps diplomacy rare and honest.

## People here

- [[Maud Pell]] — Keeper of Charts, effectively the Order's leader since the old abbot stopped leaving the dry cloister.

## Tensions

- The archive now contains its own refutation: Maud's blight survey shows the silt lines have moved off the master charts. Publishing rewrites tide-law; concealing betrays the Order's one vow, which is to measure honestly.
- Both [[Ostreve]] and the Floodsworn have begun courting the Order. Neutrality was easy when nobody asked.

## Connections

- Neap-tide footpath from [[The Mirrormoor]]; chart-copies travel under seal to [[Ostreve]] each new year.
- The bells that broke in [[The Breaking of the Bells]] were cast here, and the Order remembers every name the storm took.

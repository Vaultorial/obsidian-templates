# The Ebb Court

The law of the Tide Courts: a bench of chart-lawyers in [[Ostreve]] who rule on when land exists and who may use it while it does. It claims to be for everyone's survival, and once, plainly, it was.

## What it wants

- Stated: tide-law applied without fear or favor, because a coast this dangerous survives on predictability or not at all.
- Real, in the institution's bones: the continuity of Ostreve's position. The Court has confused the law's survival with the city's comfort for at least a generation — the Drowning schedule is where the confusion shows.

## How it works

- Authority flows from the charts: sealed yearly readings delivered by the [[Order of the Drowned Bell]], turned into rulings, licenses and the sluice calendar by the Tidemistress and her bench.
- Money flows from tolls and licenses — [[The Causeway of Bells]], moss-beds on [[The Mirrormoor]], weirs and anchorages everywhere.
- Enforcement is thin by design: wardens, clerks and the coast's deep habit of obedience. The Court has never had to compel a city before. It may be about to learn how.

## Key members

- [[Elowen Hark]] — Tidemistress; the bench's rigor made flesh.
- [[Verity Ostreve]] — ceremonial heir of the founding house, and the Court's unsuspected internal opposition.
- [[Cole Rowe]] — charter warden of the causeway; the Court's most loyal officer and least reliable instrument against Lowmark.

## Fault lines

- The charts themselves: if [[Maud Pell]]'s corrections are right, the bench has been ruling on a sea that no longer exists.
- Hark's doubt, Verity's treason, Rowe's divided heart — the Court's three strongest people are each, privately, half out the door.
- [[The Drowning of Lowmark]]: enforcing it may cost the obedience that is the Court's real currency.

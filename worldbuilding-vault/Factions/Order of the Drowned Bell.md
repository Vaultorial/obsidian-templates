# Order of the Drowned Bell

The measurers: monks of a half-sunk abbey who have sounded the Shallow Sea for three centuries and sworn exactly one vow — to record it honestly. Every chart [[The Ebb Court]] rules by begins as their ink.

## What it wants

- Stated: nothing but the work. Soundings taken, registers kept, sealed readings delivered each new year. The Order's power has always rested on wanting nothing.
- Real, since the blight survey: a way to stay honest without becoming kingmakers. Their newest measurements unmake the master charts, and there is no neutral way to say so.

## How it works

- Brothers and sisters in rotation: tide-poles, lead-lines, storm registers; the flooded cloisters of [[The Drowned Abbey]] as a wax-sealed archive.
- Funded by chart-fees from [[Ostreve]] and small sales of almanacs to fishermen — deliberately too poor to be worth corrupting.
- Neutrality by architecture: the abbey can only be reached on foot at neap tide, so nobody arrives in force or in a hurry.

## Key members

- [[Maud Pell]] — Keeper of Charts and the Order's effective head; her sealed, unsent letters to the Tidemistress are the most dangerous documents in the world.
- The old abbot — failing in the dry cloister; his last instruction was "measure twice", which Maud has decided to apply to consequences as well.

## Fault lines

- The vow against the outcome: publishing honest numbers may shatter the law that keeps the coast's people behaving predictably around a lethal sea.
- Courtship from both shores — [[Elowen Hark]] needs the Order's authority, [[Bren Callow]] needs its evidence, and the Order has no practice at being needed.

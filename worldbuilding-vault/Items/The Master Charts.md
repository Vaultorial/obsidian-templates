# The Master Charts

Twelve vellum sheets in a locked press in [[Ostreve]] that decide, by law, when every acre of the coast exists. Whoever can say what the charts mean holds the Tide Courts.

## What they are

- Twelve sheets, one for each month of the tide year, each as wide as a door and drawn in the Order's iron-gall ink. The silt lines are inked in red and the king-tide lines in black.
- Clerks copy them for every court case, but only the originals have the force of law. The copies are known, a little bitterly, as the wet copies.

## What they can and cannot do

- Nothing magical. The charts' power is that every sluice calendar, toll and Drowning in the bay is calculated from them, and no court will hear an argument against their lines.
- What they cannot do is change. By [[The Ebb Court]]'s own precedent, only a fresh sounding sealed by the [[Order of the Drowned Bell]] can amend a sheet, and no amendment has been sealed in living memory.

## History

- Drawn by the Order from three centuries of soundings at [[The Drowned Abbey]] and delivered under seal to Ostreve, where they have been kept since.
- After [[The Breaking of the Bells]], the Court added the bell-law crossings to the June and December sheets. That is the only change anyone alive has seen.
- [[The Mirrormoor Blight]] produced the first evidence that the red silt lines have drifted from the real seabed.

## Who wants them

- [[Elowen Hark]] needs the charts to remain unquestionable, or tide-law becomes an opinion.
- [[Bren Callow]] wants a public reading of the charts against Maud's soundings, which would prove the Drowning is scheduled on a wrong line.
- [[Maud Pell]] holds the soundings that could amend them and has not yet decided whether sealing an amendment is honesty or a coup.
- [[Verity Ostreve]] has promised Bren a joint reading as part of [[The Night Ferry Accord]], which means she needs the key to the press.

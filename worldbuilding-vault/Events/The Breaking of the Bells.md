# The Breaking of the Bells

The storm of 368 TR arrived faster than any table predicted and broke over [[The Causeway of Bells]] at mid-crossing. Two towers lost their voices — one cracked, one drowned out by wind — and the walkers between them never heard the descending peal. Dozens died on the stones, among them most of the causeway watch. A young warden named [[Cole Rowe]] survived by lashing himself to the Third Tower's door, with the tower's cracked clapper beating against the stone all night.

## What led to it

- A generation of thin toll revenue had left tower maintenance behind schedule; the Third Tower's known crack was on a repair list, not yet a priority.
- The storm itself outran the forecast tables — remembered as freak weather, though [[Maud Pell]]'s recent silt findings invite a colder reading: the sea may have begun leaving the charts even then.

## What changed

- Bell-law turned absolute: the descending peal now ends every crossing without exception, argument or rank.
- The [[Order of the Drowned Bell]] recast the broken bells and keeps the storm's register and its names; the recasting bound abbey and causeway in shared mourning.
- Rowe took the wardenship and rebuilt the watch around one promise: never again on his stones.

## Echoes

- Rowe wears the old clapper to this day; his rigidity about the peal is grief with a uniform on.
- Every argument about [[The Drowning of Lowmark]] happens in 368's shadow: the Courts know exactly what the sea does to people caught where they should not be.

# The Lowmark Petition

After a starving spring, [[Lowmark]] sent a net-mender up the cliff with a petition: delay that year's Drowning of the Lower Ward by one season, until the catch recovered. [[Bren Callow]] read it aloud on the floor of the Ebb Court — by every account, badly: he was no orator yet. [[Elowen Hark]] refused in four sentences, on precedent: the sluice calendar binds the Court as surely as it binds Lowmark, or it binds no one.

## What led to it

- The lean years after the moss trade wobbled and the catch thinned; the Lower Ward entered 372 with empty smoke-lofts.
- Genuine faith, on Lowmark's side, that the Court would bend for hunger. The petition was respectful to the point of meekness — a detail the pamphlets now omit.

## What changed

- The Drowning proceeded on schedule. The Lower Ward moved out, the ward flooded, the precedent held.
- That night, in a net-shed, the refusal's witnesses swore the oath that founded [[The Floodsworn]]: *no more drownings*. Bren left Ostreve a failed petitioner and arrived home a leader.
- Hark's four sentences became the most-reprinted text in [[Hethwick]]'s press history — usually without the fifth, in which she ordered Court grain released to Lowmark. Both sides find the fifth sentence inconvenient.

## Echoes

- Every Floodsworn strategy since is an argument about this day: petition again, properly — or stop asking.
- [[Elowen Hark]] has reread her own ruling more often than anyone in Lowmark has. Precedent held; the coast did not.

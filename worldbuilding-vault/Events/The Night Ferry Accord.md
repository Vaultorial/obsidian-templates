# The Night Ferry Accord

On a fog-shut autumn night of 373 TR, with the causeway under water, [[Wick the Ferryman]] carried a hooded fare from [[Ostreve]] to no destination at all: the boat circled the bay for three hours while [[Verity Ostreve]] and [[Bren Callow]] argued in whispers over a shuttered lamp. They landed with an accord neither could afford to sign in daylight, named — by Wick, dryly — after the only territory it was made in.

## What led to it

- The hungry winter after [[The Mirrormoor Blight]] put the dike-breaker wing within reach of acting alone; Bren needed something real to hold his own oath together.
- Verity had concluded the same season that the sluice vote, once called, would pass — and that the only winnable fight was over *when* it was called.

## What changed

- The terms: Verity delays the sluice vote by every procedural means her position allows; Bren keeps the dikes unbroken while the delay holds; both work toward forcing a joint reading of the charts — Court and Lowmark witnesses together — before any vote.
- Two letters exist, one in each conspirator's keeping, in deliberately deniable hands. Wick witnessed both and holds neither.
- For half a year it has worked: no vote called, no dike broken. The accord is the quiet load-bearing wall of the present peace.

## Echoes

- Everything now rests on two reputations that cannot survive disclosure and one ferryman's silence.
- The joint chart-reading the accord aims at would summon [[Maud Pell]] and her survey to the floor of the Court — which is exactly the collision she has been postponing. See [[The Drowning of Lowmark]].

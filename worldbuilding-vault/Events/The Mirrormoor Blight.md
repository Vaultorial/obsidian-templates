# The Mirrormoor Blight

In the wet spring of 373 TR the eastern pearl-moss beds greyed, slimed and died inside a season. [[The Mirrormoor]]'s farmers lost harvest, licenses' worth and winter stores in one blow; the grading-halls of [[Lowmark]] stood half empty and the Lower Ward went into the cold months already hungry. [[Perrin Lowsmith]]'s family beds were among the first taken.

## What led to it

- Nothing the farmers did — which made it worse. Moss-farming had not changed in three generations.
- The sea had. [[Maud Pell]] walked the dead beds with Perrin as guide, sampled silt and salinity along the channel margins, and found the lines of fresh and salt shifted well off the master charts: the blight followed the new lines exactly.

## What changed

- The moor's eastern licenses became paper that describes a sea that no longer exists — tide-law's first open encounter with its own obsolescence, though only a handful of people understand that yet.
- Lowmark's hungry winter hardened [[The Floodsworn]] and recruited its quartermaster: Perrin came off the moor with nothing to farm and a talent for lists.
- Maud's survey ledger became the most consequential unpublished document in the Tide Courts. She has told the whole truth to no one — not yet [[Elowen Hark]], not quite Perrin.

## Echoes

- The blight is why this midsummer's [[The Drowning of Lowmark]] falls on people with no reserves — the cruelty is compound.
- The silt finding reframes everything backward, too: even [[The Breaking of the Bells]] reads differently if the sea has been drifting off the charts for decades.

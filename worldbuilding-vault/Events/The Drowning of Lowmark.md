# The Drowning of Lowmark

The scheduled event the whole vault leans toward: at the midsummer king tide of 374 TR, by the standing sluice calendar, the Lower Ward of [[Lowmark]] is to be evacuated and flooded, easing the king tide's load off the dike ring that protects the harbor channel — and, across the bay, [[Ostreve]]'s undercliff. The calendar calls it maintenance. Lowmark calls it by its older, honest name.

## The mechanism

Tide-law treats the Drowning as settled procedure: notice posted, ward cleared, sluices opened, waters guided in and out with the tide, ward re-occupied after the silt is shoveled. It has been done within living memory without a death — and without a choice. That last part is what 374 will not forgive.

## What stands between now and the water

- The vote: the sluice order must be confirmed by the Ebb Court bench. [[Verity Ostreve]] has delayed the calling of it for half a year under [[The Night Ferry Accord]]; midsummer ends every delaying tactic at once.
- The oath: [[Bren Callow]] has held the dike-breakers to the accord. If the vote passes, "no more drownings" stops being a petition and becomes an instruction.
- The charts: [[Maud Pell]]'s unpublished silt survey suggests the dike arithmetic itself may be out of date — the Drowning might no longer even do what it claims to do. A joint chart-reading before the vote is the one outcome every conspirator wants and no institution has agreed to.
- The crossing: whatever moves that week — evacuees, wardens, mobs, grain — moves over [[The Causeway of Bells]] or aboard [[Wick the Ferryman]]'s boat. [[Cole Rowe]] and Wick have already, without a word exchanged, begun provisioning for the worst week of their lives. [[Perrin Lowsmith]]'s barrels are stacked and his billet-lists written.

## Ways it can break

- The vote passes and the ward drowns on schedule: the Floodsworn's hard wing inherits the movement, and the Courts learn what an instruction looks like.
- The vote is forced open by the survey: tide-law survives by amending itself in public — the narrow, brightest path.
- The dikes are broken first: the king tide arrives unmanaged, and 368 looks, in hindsight, like a rehearsal.
